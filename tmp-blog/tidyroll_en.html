Short answer: iPhone photos are usually saved as HEIC. When Windows won't open them, or a website or office system won't accept them, convert them to JPG. For a whole batch, Tidyroll is free, uploads nothing and keeps the date each photo was taken. For a few photos, use "Save as" in the Photos app; to make future photos JPG, change one setting on the iPhone.

What HEIC is, and why it causes trouble on Windows

HEIC is the image format Apple uses by default on the iPhone. It looks about the same as JPG at a smaller file size. That's fine on the iPhone, but on Windows:

  • Photos won't open or show no thumbnail if the extension that reads HEIC isn't installed.
  • Uploads get rejected: many application forms, print services and older programs accept only JPG.
  • Live Photo videos get mixed in: copying from an iPhone brings a short .MOV clip with every Live Photo, so your photo folder fills up with videos.

Which method fits (4 ways compared)

For family photos and anything with location data, the methods that don't upload (Tidyroll, the Photos app, the iPhone setting) are the safe choice.

Method 1: Convert a batch with Tidyroll (no upload)

Tidyroll is a free Windows app for tidying up iPhone photos (made by Daily Picks). It turns HEIC into JPG and keeps each photo's date. Everything runs on your PC; nothing goes to the internet.

  1. Copy the photos from your iPhone to the PC (connect the cable and copy in File Explorer, or download them from iCloud Photos).
  2. Get "Tidyroll" from the Microsoft Store (). You can also use it without installing by opening the Tidyroll page in your browser.
  3. Open Tidyroll and drag the photo folder onto it (or choose the photos).
  4. The report shows how many HEIC photos, videos and Live Photo videos it found. Change the JPG quality if you like (92 to start with) and click "Convert and save".
  5. Right-click the new zip › "Extract All". The JPGs come out with the date they were taken, and the date is also written inside each photo.

What it does: converts HEIC to JPG, copies JPGs, other images and videos as they are, keeps the date both inside the photo and as the file date, and can keep the original HEIC files too (an option).
Free and Pro: the free version converts up to 20 HEIC photos per run. Tidyroll Pro in the Store version (one-time purchase, about $4) removes the limit and adds year/month folders, separate Live Photo video folders, duplicate detection and removing location data.

Method 2: Save one at a time in the Photos app

For just a few photos, the Windows Photos app can save them as JPG.

  1. Double-click the HEIC photo to open it in Photos. If it won't open, install "HEIF Image Extensions" (free) from the Microsoft Store and, if needed, "HEVC Video Extensions" (paid). The newer Photos app in Windows 11 is starting to open HEIC without them.
  2. Open the "…" menu, choose "Save as", set the file type to JPG and save.

It's one photo at a time, so dozens of photos take a while.

Method 3: Change an iPhone setting (JPG from now on)

  • Take photos as JPG: on the iPhone, go to Settings › Camera › Formats and choose "Most Compatible". New photos will be JPG (larger files). Existing photos don't change.
  • Convert when copying to a PC: in Settings › Photos, under "Transfer to Mac or PC", choose "Automatic". Photos copied over the cable are handed over in a format Windows can open.

Method 4: An online converter

Searching "HEIC to JPG" turns up plenty of sites that convert in the browser. They're handy, but most upload your photos to their servers. Photos can contain the place they were taken, so these sites aren't a good fit for family or home photos.

If it doesn't work

  • After converting, the date became today: some converters set the file date to the conversion date. Tidyroll writes the date taken both inside the photo and as the file date. Sort by "Date taken".
  • Some HEIC files couldn't be read: damaged files or unusual HEIC files made by other apps may not convert. Tidyroll copies those files as they are and tells you how many.
  • A photo is turned sideways: iPhone photos store their rotation separately. If a converted JPG is sideways, right-click it in File Explorer › "Rotate right".
  • Videos (.MOV) won't play: iPhone videos are often HEVC, which on Windows may need "HEVC Video Extensions".

FAQ

Q. Does converting reduce quality?
A. JPG changes the image very slightly. At the default setting (92) you're unlikely to see a difference. Raise it to 95–100 if you prefer (files get larger).

Q. What happens to the location data?
A. It's kept unless you remove it. For photos you share, Tidyroll Pro's "Remove location from the photos" is the safe choice.

Q. Does it work on a Mac?
A. The Store version is for Windows. On a Mac, the built-in Preview app can export to JPG.
